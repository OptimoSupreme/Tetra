# Potential names

Delta: The Greek symbol for change, mathematically represented by a triangle. It’s highly technical, slightly military-industrial, and fits the logo perfectly.

Crux: A sharp, aggressive word meaning the decisive core of a problem. It sounds like a high-end weapons or tech manufacturer.

Apex: The peak or tip of a triangle. It’s a classic high-performance moniker that sounds right at home on a custom Linux build or a machined panel.

Prism: Sleek, optical, and scientific. It implies light, clarity, and high-level physics, fitting nicely with a triangular shape.

Truss: In engineering, a truss is a framework—almost always consisting of triangles—used to support heavy structures like bridges or roofs. It sounds incredibly industrial, deeply rooted in physical reality, and perfectly explains the strength of a triangular logo.

Vertex: The exact point where two or more lines meet to form an angle (like the corners of a triangle). It has a highly technical, sharp sound that fits perfectly on a milled CNC chassis or embedded in a network topology diagram.

Tetra: Short for tetrahedron (a three-dimensional triangle/pyramid). It sounds like a massive, heavy-duty Earth corporation (e.g., "Tetra Dynamics") that builds hardware designed to take a beating in the deep mines of Hoxxes IV.

Tectonic: Relates to the massive, structural plates of the Earth's crust. It implies your creations are foundational, immensely powerful, and built on a planetary scale. A triangular logo with "TECTONIC" next to it looks heavy and premium.

Lithos: The Greek word for stone. It carries that Asuran "floating stone architecture" vibe while remaining a completely Earth-born, historical word.

Mantle: The structural, load-bearing layer of the Earth right beneath the crust. It implies a solid, unshakeable foundation for whatever system it's running on.

Axiom: A statement or proposition that is regarded as an established, accepted, or self-evidently true foundation. It sounds like a high-end, mathematically flawless tech manufacturer.

Vector: A quantity having direction as well as magnitude, almost universally represented by an arrow or a triangle. It’s sleek, fast, and highly technical.

Datum: A singular piece of information, or a fixed starting point of a scale or operation. It implies absolute precision and a zero-tolerance policy for engineering errors.
